## Classified `unclear` (11) — needs your adjudication

These are the ones the rules genuinely cannot settle. They share a shape:
a parcel of a wine several years old, with neither release nor back-vintage
language anywhere in subject or body.

| Offer | Gap | Wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2020 Ch. Margaux | 6 | "we have access to a top-tier parcel" |
| 2019 Ch. Pichon-Baron | 7 | "since trying it on release" |
| 2019 Vieux Château Certan | 7 | "we have secured a small parcel" |
| 2018 Cheval des Andes | 8 | "our buyer has secured a small parcel" |
| 2021 Tignanello | 5 | "secured a further parcel" |
| 2022 Tignanello | 4 | "I have…" |
| 2022 Olivier Leflaive | 4 | "an impeccable parcel of 2022 whites" |
| 2017 Viña Gravonia Blanco | 9 | "a wine we don't often offer" |
| 2013 Comtes de Champagne Rosé | 13 | "today we have a delightful example" |
| 2018 Dom Pérignon | 8 | "the eagerly anticipated offer for" |
| 2019 Ch. Latour | 7 | "following the strong demand generated by the release … earlier this year" |

I pulled the full body of the 2020 Ch. Margaux to test whether bodies resolve
these. **They do not** — the body contains no tier-1 or tier-2 phrase at all,
just "top-tier parcel" and five critic notes. So `unclear` is irreducible from
language alone, not a symptom of reading too little.

Note the last four are arguably *releases* despite large gaps: Champagne,
Dom Pérignon, López de Heredia and Ch. Latour all release years after the
vintage by design. This is exactly the case that kills a vintage-age rule.

**What I need from you:** a verdict on this bucket as a whole. If offers like
the 2020 Margaux are worth having, `unclear` should default to captured and
labelled. If they are noise, it should default to captured and labelled
`back_vintage`. Either way nothing is dropped — but the default decides how
much hand-sorting you do later.

## Defects found in the spec

### 1. Triage must be per message, not per thread

`Christopher.Parker@bbr.com` replies into the automated order-confirmation
thread from `bbr@bbr.com`, minutes after it arrives, with a genuine offer. The
11 August 2026 instance carries the Prager release; the 12 March 2026 instance
carries the 2023 Tignanello release.

This has happened **five times** (31 Oct 2024, 17 Jan 2025, 19 Jan 2026,
12 Mar 2026, 11 Aug 2026) — roughly two offers a year. A thread-level triage
keyed on the first sender drops every one of them silently.

### 2. The sender shape rule caught a typo, and an allowlist would not have

On 30 July 2026 an offer arrived from **`christopher.pakrer@bbr.com`** —
"pakrer". The follow-up from the correct address says "My email address was
incorrectly input on the last email". A hardcoded allowlist would have dropped
it; the `firstname.lastname@bbr.com` shape rule admits it.

That pair is also a near-duplicate: the same Lingua Franca offer sent twice,
the second subject suffixed "UPDATE". Content fingerprints will not dedupe it,
because the bodies differ by an apology paragraph.

### 3. Extraction hazards confirmed in the current mail

From the 2020 Ch. Margaux body:

- `3-bottle cases of2020 Château Margaux` — the name runs into the *preceding*
  word this time, not the price. Splitting on `£` is not sufficient on its own.
- `£1,020 per 3-bottle case in bond` — thousands separator inside the amount.
- `3-bottle case` — a format the existing case-size vocabulary should be
  checked against.

## Recommended changes before a second run

1. Add `ex-bodega` and generalise the `ex-<origin>` family in tier 1.
2. Make triage message-level, and explicitly include messages whose thread
   begins with a rejected sender.
3. Decide the `unclear` default (above).
4. Treat "secured a parcel of" as a **weak** back-vintage signal only when the
   gap exceeds about five years — it appears in six of the eleven unclear
   cases and nowhere in the 58 releases.

## What was not tested

Extraction and staging. This run exercised triage and classification only, so
nothing here validates the row shape, price parsing or the ingestion RPC chain.
A second supervised run should extract a batch and stop before accepting it.
